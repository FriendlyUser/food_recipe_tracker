## Stovetop Timings vs. Instant Pot Efficiency

Traditional stovetop soups require constant monitoring and hours of simmering to build a rich broth. The Instant Pot drastically cuts down the time while pressure-locking the savory rotisserie flavors directly into the vegetables.

| Feature | Traditional Stovetop | Instant Pot (Recommended) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Prep Time** | 20 mins | 10 mins |
| **Total Cook Time** | 1.5–2 Hours | **20 mins** |
| **Broth Depth** | Good | Rich & Concentrated |
| **Best For** | Lazy weekends | Fast weeknight dinners |

> [!NOTE]
> Using a pre-cooked **Costco Rotisserie Chicken** saves you the hassle of handling raw meat. The residual seasonings from the chicken immediately elevate the broth without needing extra bouillon cubes.

## The Core Ingredients

Keep it incredibly simple. You only need a few staple items to make this work:

* **Costco Chicken** (Pre-cooked rotisserie)
* **Carrot**
* **Onion**
* **Garlic**
* **Noodles**
* **Instant Pot**

---

## My Go-To Instant Pot Workflow

Here is the most efficient, low-mess way to prep and cook this soup:

1. **Strip the Chicken:** Start by stripping the meat from the Costco chicken directly in the Instant Pot insert. Discard the bones and skin (or leave the bones in for a richer bone broth if you plan to strain it later).
2. **Soak the Noodles:** Place your dry noodles in a separate bowl of warm water to soak. This softens them and removes excess starch while you prep the rest of the meal.
3. **Prep the Veggies:**
* Peel the **carrot**, then cut it into thick coins.
* Peel the **garlic** cloves and remove the hard ends.
* Remove the skin from the **onion** and roughly chop it.
* Toss all the prepped vegetables directly into the pot with the chicken.


4. **Cook:** Add water (or chicken broth) to cover the ingredients. Seal the lid and set the Instant Pot to pressure cook (or "bake" if using a combo setting) to lock in the flavors.

---

## The Golden Rule: Noodles Cook Separately

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Never pressure cook the noodles with the soup.**
> **Always soak or cook your noodles separately.**
> If you leave noodles in the Instant Pot during a high-pressure cycle, they will turn into complete mush and absorb all your hard-earned broth. Simply drain your soaked noodles and stir them directly into the hot, finished soup right before serving!
